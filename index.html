Simulation assumptions & research sources — adjust walking and robot speeds

Adjust the scenario

Human 1.20 m/s is a scenario assumption, not a universal measured warehouse speed. The default robot rates are illustrative and below the selected brochure's specifications. Published speeds are not guaranteed trip averages.

Time assumptions: 6 s to pick one item on either side; 4 s lift (brochure); 4 s lower and 1 s station clearance (assumed). Travel duration = route distance ÷ the relevant speed. The drawing is schematic, using 0.12 m per SVG unit for travel accounting; rack icons are not dimensionally scaled.

What this model includes

Same rack locations, three different SKUs and one picker per side. The manual route is the shortest of the six SKU sequences on the drawn aisle network. The worker collects all items before returning. No extra manual search penalty is added.

Each robot carries one shelf. Available robots retrieve in parallel, wait in assigned staging bays, and enter the shared station in arrival order. Station admission, picking and exit are exclusive. A robot becomes available again after returning and lowering its shelf.

Order ready: all three items are at the station. System reset: the manual worker has returned, or every robot has returned and lowered its shelf. Local reaching, packing, charging, acceleration, detailed intersection control and aisle congestion are excluded. Buffers and dispatch are illustrative, not an exact Geek+ RMS implementation.

Research: Geek+'s P800R sheet specifies empty travel at 2.3 m/s, loaded travel at 2.0 m/s and lifting at 4 s; specifications vary by generation. The official Shelf-to-Person workflow uses fleet dispatch, shelf delivery, operator confirmation and shelf return. Queue-based pod delivery is also described in RMFS research.
